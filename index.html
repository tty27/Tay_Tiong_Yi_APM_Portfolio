From a coding project
to a product problem.

IgnitionIQ began as a car quiz idea. The important shift was recognising that a game alone wasn't enough. The project needed a clear user problem, a reason to return and a product experience that made learning feel rewarding.

01

The problem

Automotive knowledge is often consumed passively. I wanted to explore how a digital experience could make learning about cars more interactive and motivating.

02

Target users

Young people and automotive enthusiasts who enjoy cars but want a lightweight, entertaining way to test and build their knowledge.

03

Product goal

Create a simple loop: choose a challenge, answer, receive feedback, improve your score and have a reason to try again.

04

Product decisions

Three game modes cover car recognition, traffic rules and driving situations. Levels, hints, scoring and leaderboards add progression and replayability.

Build the core loop before adding complexity.

For an early product, I would prioritise a strong quiz experience, clear feedback and progression before secondary features.

P0 Core quiz + scoringP0 Progression / levelsP1 Hints + leaderboardP2 Social / advanced features
05

Technical constraints

I had to balance product ambition with what could realistically be designed and implemented within the project scope.

06

What I learned

The most interesting part wasn't writing the code. It was deciding what the product should do, what mattered most and how the pieces should work together.
